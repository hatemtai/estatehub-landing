The parts that take months.

Any developer can build a grid of listings. What slows a marketplace down is search that feels instant, deals that run for weeks, proving who people are, and moving money. Those four are finished here.

  1. Search that lives in the address bar

    Buyers move around a map of price pins, open a filter panel, or type what they want in plain words. Whatever they do is written to the URL, so the back button works and a search can be sent to a partner as a link.

    • Leaflet on OpenStreetMap tiles. No map key, no map bill.
    • Radius search from a dropped pin or the visitor's own location, up to 50 km
    • Results refresh 400 ms after the typing stops
    • AI search shows what it understood and what it could not apply

  2. A sale that takes weeks, tracked in one place

    A buyer reserves, sends proof of payment, then waits while documents are checked and ownership changes hands. Each stage has its own checklist. The buyer sees where things stand, and your team ticks items off from the admin console.

    • Separate flows for buying and for leasing
    • Stages and steps come from the server, so you can change them without a release
    • A blocked step carries a note explaining why

  3. Know who you are dealing with

    Customers verify their identity in six steps: personal details, address, an ID document, a selfie, declarations and a final review. Sellers apply with their business details. Both land in one queue for your team.

    • Email and phone are verified alongside the documents
    • Every request shows where it stands: pending, under review, approved or rejected

  4. Money, seen from both sides

    A seller sees what each property earned, what the platform kept and what is ready to withdraw. On your side, staff verify incoming bank transfers, record payments made in person, and send approved payouts as a batch.

    • Commission and reservation rules are set once, in admin
    • Payments export to CSV

  5. Messaging that behaves like a messaging app

    Buyers and sellers talk over WebSockets, so messages, typing indicators and read receipts arrive as they happen. Start a conversation from a listing and it follows you around the site in a small dock while you keep browsing.

    • Image attachments and reply templates
    • A notification centre for customers, sellers and admins
    • Customers choose which notifications they receive

  6. Rebrand it from the admin console

    The platform name, logo, favicon and brand colours are settings, loaded from the API at runtime. Change them, save, and the public site and both portals pick them up. Nobody has to open the code or redeploy.

    • Storage, email, bank details and social sign-in are set up the same way
    • Saved keys are write-only. The console shows that one is set, never what it is.

One app, three audiences
Customers, sellers and admins each get their own layout, navigation and route guard. They share one portal shell, so a fix made once reaches all three.
Server-rendered where it counts
Listing pages are resolved on the server, with metadata and structured data in the first response. Search and the portals run on the client.

Before you buy.

Short answers to the practical questions.

What do I need to run it?

Node.js 20.9 or newer and pnpm. Copy the example environment file, point it at your EstateHub API, and run the dev command. That API URL is the only setting it cannot start without.

Where can I host it?

Anywhere that runs Next.js. Vercel is the shortest path. On your own server, build it, start it, and put Nginx or Caddy in front for TLS.

How do buyers pay?

By bank transfer. The buyer uploads proof of payment and your team verifies it in the admin console. Payments made in person can be recorded by an admin. There is no card gateway built in.

Can I rebrand it without touching code?

Yes, for the name, logo, favicon and brand colours. They are settings in the admin console. To go further, the colour, radius and spacing tokens are in a single stylesheet.

Do the maps cost anything?

No. The map is Leaflet on OpenStreetMap tiles, which need no key and no billing account.

Is dark mode everywhere?

In the seller portal and the admin console, yes. The public site and the customer account are light only. The design system document lists the steps to switch them on if you want it.

Where do API keys and credentials go?

Into the admin console, under Settings, then Integrations. That covers social sign-in, storage, email, bank transfer details and the AI provider. A saved key is never sent back to the browser.

Which AI providers work with AI search?

OpenAI, Anthropic and Gemini. Pick one, enter a model and a key, and switch it on. With it off, the normal search and filters are unaffected.
